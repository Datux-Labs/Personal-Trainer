Expand a guide

The lifting supports your activities. It should not compete with every one of them for maximum effort.

Rebuild a repeatable running base
  • Start: Monday 20–25 minutes; Saturday 30–40 minutes. Walk breaks count. Keep the effort conversational, around 3–4/10.
  • After at least two comfortable exposures to a duration with normal next-day function, consider adding five minutes to one run only. Hold other training increases steady.
  • Later possibilities: Monday 30–40 minutes and Saturday 60–75 minutes. Consider 75–90 minutes only after several stable weeks—not now.
  • Saturday depends on recovery from Friday volleyball and the demands of pickleball later that day. Shorten the run when needed.
  • Two weekly runs are not a promise of half-marathon readiness. A race-specific block may require a third short run and reducing another activity.
Swim with breaks, not a distance test
  • Start with 15–20 elapsed minutes, including rest: roughly 6–8 comfortable short lengths, usually 25 yards/meters each.
  • Rest 30–60 seconds or longer until breathing settles. If a length is not comfortable, choose a suitable supervised shorter-distance setting or a beginner lesson.
  • After two relaxed sessions at the same dose, add 1–2 repeats or lengthen one repeat—not both while cutting rest.
  • Eventually, 20–30 easy minutes including breaks may fit. Swimming adds shoulder work; it is not automatically recovery between climbing and volleyball.
  • No paddles, sprint sets or breath-holding challenges.
Strength progression without the extra fatigue
  • Use familiar loads and finish cleanly before failure. Start Monday squats with about three good reps left; keep Thursday at 3–4 reps in reserve.
  • Build clean reps within the range before increasing load. A third squat set is a later option only if the whole week remains sustainable.
  • Power work is brief, early and only when fresh. Reset fully between jumps. No bonus plyometric circuit or hard bike intervals are required.
  • Boulder first on Wednesday; separate lifting by several hours when practical. Pulldowns are only a substitute when the climb is canceled.
  • No routine heavy extra vertical pulling, curls, hangboarding or grip finishers. Mobility uses a controlled, comfortable range.
Recovery is part of the schedule
  • Tuesday is protected. No training is required. Optional gentle mobility is not a catch-up session.
  • Eleven sessions across six active days is a starting template—not a guarantee of recoverability. Separate double sessions by several hours when practical.
  • Wednesday-to-Thursday upper-body work and Thursday-to-Friday leg work are compromises. Shoulder, elbow, grip or leg fatigue is a reason to reduce work.
  • Prioritize 7–9 hours of sleep when possible. Reduce optional jumps and accessories before adding more work; do not force every box after an exhausting sport session.
  • Persistent pain or symptoms that affect normal movement need appropriate professional assessment, not an automatic exercise swap.
Golf, volleyball & pickleball
  • Friday range: 30–40 minutes including a gentle warm-up, ideally several hours before beach volleyball. Roughly 30–40 balls or fewer—not a quota.
  • Start with wedges, use mostly controlled mid-irons, and add a few longer-club shots only while fresh. Pick one target or technique cue and reset between shots.
  • No extra golf-specific circuit or maximum-speed swing work. Shorten or skip the range for back, shoulder, elbow or wrist fatigue.
  • Saturday pickleball: keep your usual moderate session, not an extra conditioning workout. Shorten the earlier run if Friday volleyball leaves residual fatigue.
  • Sunday bouldering includes a recovery check. Shorten the climb when needed.
Winter rule: replace, don’t stack
  • Use the This week selector above. Saturday snowboarding replaces the longer run and pickleball. A full riding weekend replaces Sunday bouldering too.
  • Before a demanding trip, reduce Thursday lower-body volume and omit gym jumps. Shorten or skip Friday golf/volleyball for recovery or travel.
  • Monday afterward is a recovery decision—not an obligation to repay missed training. Two short lifts may fit a demanding riding week better than forcing three.
  • Optional preseason wall-sits can replace an accessory set, starting around 2 × 20–30 seconds. They are not bonus volume.
  • Frequent winter interruptions to the longer run mean reassessing the half-marathon goal, not claiming both schedules fit unchanged.
